# Editorial collaboration

## Start with the arc

State what the viewer should understand/feel/do. Distinguish the normal subject
of a community or show from a special day's activity. Use evidence-backed
positioning, not unsupported growth or outcome claims. Prefer existing speech;
new narration is an option to discuss, not a default.

Read the complete source before shortlisting. For a reel, seek hook → purpose →
concrete example → benefit/invitation. For a topical/long-form cut, preserve setup,
exploration, disagreement, conclusion, and necessary qualifications. Do not force
one story structure onto every video.

## Numbered candidates

Give stable numbers, source ID/date, approximate range, actual quote, and one-line
purpose/caveat. Say if a quote is abbreviated. Do not silently renumber after a
rejection. Record shortlisted/rejected/approved-to-use separately. “Interesting”
means audition-worthy, not final approval.

Cue the chosen original passage with a small lead-in; report time and a brief
label, not a long analysis on every “next.” If the user questions the timestamp,
verify the actual source/clock and quote before retrying. Do not guess a new time.

## Workshop the transcript

Show the exact proposed speech with speaker turns. Mark omitted internal words
and separate source excerpts from bridge-card copy. Keep an editable transcript
for annotation; preserve the user's annotations and reread them before revising.
Include clip IDs, camera, intent, source ranges, and draft/output times when known.

For “keep until X,” record the last intended spoken words and whether X is
excluded. If ambiguous, clarify. Do not rewrite what someone said into smoother
language that isn't in the recording. Removing a qualification or stitching a
response to a different question needs special scrutiny.

## Short previews before full rebuilds

When wording or a seam is uncertain, make an isolated **video** stitch with the
same camera/audio relationship intended for the reel. Test actual prosody, final
syllables, and changes between speakers. Then integrate the accepted sequence.
Do not treat “work on a transcript here” as permission to render.

Alternatives are useful when a real decision remains (homepage vs signup, normal
vs tighter cut); don't automatically produce two versions of everything. Stop
rendering rejected branches. Apply global copy changes globally (e.g. remove a
phrase from every card), not just where convenient.

## Record decisions, not bureaucracy

Update `PROJECT.md` after meaningful decisions or builds: current draft, pending
edits, selections, exact approved card text, explicit stop boundaries, known QA
limits. Keep immutable draft snapshots. Explicit authorization for a preview can
combine planning/rendering gates; no need to demand approval of every timestamp.
A substantive new direction still merits a proposal.

Finish with the actual link, runtime, changes, and unresolved issues. If requested,
open the video in the player and use a completion sound/`say` on macOS. Distinguish
an accepted open command, a verified document/time, and actual playback evidence.
